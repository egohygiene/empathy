## Introduction

Generative audio systems increasingly allow users to express musical intent
through natural language and other high-level controls. At the same time,
research in music psychology, affective computing, adaptive music, and adjacent
clinical contexts suggests that musical experiences can interact meaningfully
with subjective state. What remains unclear is whether a person's own
interpretable semantic vocabulary for desired internal transitions can be
mapped through generative audio into measurable acoustic realizations and then
refined longitudinally from observed response.

This manuscript currently treats that question as a research direction rather
than an established finding. The originating N-of-1 catharsis observation is a
hypothesis generator only.

## Research Gap

_Current hypothesis:_ existing work spans affect regulation, state sensing,
generative music, and closed-loop adaptation, but the novelty boundary must be
verified against primary literature before a contribution claim is frozen.

A candidate contribution is an interpretable, user-extensible semantic control
layer that records:

1. intended semantic controls;
2. the acoustic realization actually generated;
3. subjective and optionally physiological response;
4. longitudinal relationships between those variables;
5. uncertainty and provenance.

## Limitations

The current concept does not establish treatment efficacy, universal mappings
between audio and mental states, or specific neurological mechanisms. Mental
health states must not be reduced to simplistic brain-region mappings, and
traditional or contemplative ontologies must not be represented as established
neuroanatomy.

## Future Work

Potential extensions include multi-participant studies, physiological sensing,
wearable signals, EEG, richer acoustic analysis, adaptive personalization, and
more expressive generative controls. These are extensions, not prerequisites
for the first paper.
