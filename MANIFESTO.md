# Empathy Manifesto

## Opening Declaration

A repository is not only files, workflows, and checks. It is a place where people meet systems,
expectations, one another, and the consequences of what gets built.

We believe that foundation deserves care.

## What We Believe

We believe clarity is a form of kindness.

We believe automation should extend human capability without erasing human authority.

We believe quality is not the absence of visible findings; it is the willingness to see, understand,
and act on evidence.

We believe reusable systems should preserve difference. A common foundation should make distinct
projects easier to sustain, not make every project the same.

We believe accessibility, privacy, security, maintenance, and respectful participation are part of
engineering—not work to defer until after the “real” system is finished.

## What We Commit To

We commit to making important behavior explicit.

We commit to keeping the universal core small and optional capabilities honest.

We commit to naming uncertainty, preserving provenance, and correcting the architecture when the
evidence changes.

We commit to reviewable changes, recoverable workflows, least privilege, and meaningful paths to
contest or override automation.

We commit to welcoming both people and useful tools without pretending they have the same needs,
authority, or accountability.

## What We Refuse to Normalize

We refuse to normalize opaque automation, performative compliance, inaccessible contribution paths,
silent policy drift, or pressure to hide legitimate findings for a green check.

We refuse to treat contributors as throughput, users as profiles, or maintainers as inexhaustible.

We refuse to call a staged capability active, an inapplicable tool passing, an assumption a fact, or
an AI-generated claim verified merely because it sounds complete.

## Invitation

Empathy is an invitation to build repository foundations that are rigorous without becoming cold,
opinionated without becoming coercive, and reusable without becoming monolithic.

Adopt what is useful. Question what is not. Preserve the reasons. Improve the system with evidence
and care.

## Closing Statement

Healthy software begins before the first feature. It begins with the conditions under which people
and systems are asked to work together.

## Provenance and Alignment

This manifesto translates the commitments in [`PURPOSE.md`](PURPOSE.md), [`VISION.md`](VISION.md),
[`PRINCIPLES.md`](PRINCIPLES.md), and [`PILLARS.md`](PILLARS.md) into public cultural language. It is
not an enforceable policy, contribution guide, or roadmap.
